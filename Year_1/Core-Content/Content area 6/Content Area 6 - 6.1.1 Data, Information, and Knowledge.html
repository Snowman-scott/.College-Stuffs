6.1.1: Understanding the
Differences and Links Between
Data, Information, and Knowledge

What is Data?

Definition:

Data refers to raw, unprocessed facts and figures without any context or meaning.

It can be in the form of numbers, text, images, sounds, or symbols.

Data alone doesn't tell us much until it is organised or analysed.

Key Characteristics:

  • Unorganised and meaningless on its own
  • Can be quantitative (numerical) or qualitative (descriptive)
  • Requires processing to become useful

Real-World Examples:

  • Sales Data: 100, 200, 150
  • Weather Data: 28°C, 85% humidity
  • Survey Responses: "Yes," "No," "Maybe"

Data is like the raw ingredients in a recipe, on its own, it's not very useful until processed.

What is Information?

Definition:

Information is processed, organised, and structured data that has meaning and is useful for decision-making.

It answers questions like "Who?", "What?", "When?", and "Where?".

Key Characteristics:

  • Organised to provide clarity
  • Meaningful and relevant to the user
  • Helps in making decisions
Data + Context + Processing = Information

Real-World Examples:

  • Sales Report: Product A sold 100 units in March 2024, generating £5,000 in revenue
  • Weather Forecast: The temperature will reach 28°C with 85% humidity in London tomorrow

If data is raw ingredients, information is the cooked meal, ready to be used.

What is Knowledge?

Definition:

Knowledge is the understanding, insight, and experience gained from analysing and interpreting information.

It enables people to make decisions, predictions, and judgements based on past experiences and context.

Key Characteristics:

  • Based on interpretation and experience
  • Helps in problem-solving and decision-making
  • Can be explicit (easily shared) or tacit (personal, harder to explain)
Information + Experience + Context + Reflection = Knowledge

Real-World Examples:

  • Doctor's Diagnosis: Analyses symptoms combined with medical knowledge to diagnose illness
  • Driving Skills: Road rules + experience = knowledge of traffic situations

Knowledge is like the chef's skill, knowing how to prepare a great dish based on ingredients (data) and a recipe (information).

Links Between Data, Information, and Knowledge

The Progression:

  • Data is the foundation – You start with raw data
  • Processing creates information – Data is organised to provide meaning
  • Experience builds knowledge – Information is interpreted to gain insights for better decisions

🌐 Complete Example: Website Analytics

Data: Website receives 10,000 visits in a week (just a number).

Information: 70% of visitors are from mobile devices, and most visits happen at 8 PM.

Knowledge: Understanding that mobile users prefer evening browsing leads to the decision to optimise the website for mobile and launch evening marketing campaigns.

The DIKW Pyramid

DATA

📚 Educational Example:

Data: Raw test scores: 50, 75, 100

Information: Math test scores show range from 50 to 100

Knowledge: Students improved after extra lessons

Wisdom: Incorporate interactive teaching methods and run extra lessons during half-term breaks before exams

📝 Exam-Style Questions - Section C

Question 5 9 marks – Analyse

An e-commerce company tracks the following data points: number of website visits, products viewed, time spent on each page, items added to basket, and completed purchases.

Analyse how this data flows through the Data-Information-Knowledge hierarchy to support business decision-making. Your answer should include:

  • Examples of how raw data becomes information
  • How information leads to knowledge
  • Specific business decisions that could result

Command verb: Analyse

Separate information into components and identify their characteristics. Show how factors interact and impact the scenario.

📝 Exam-Style Questions - Section D

Question 6 12 marks – Evaluate

A software development company is deciding whether to invest £150,000 in a new data analytics platform that would automatically process raw customer data into actionable insights (information) and provide AI-driven recommendations (knowledge).

Currently, the company manually processes data using spreadsheets, which takes approximately 20 hours per week of staff time.

Evaluate the importance of moving from manual data processing to automated information and knowledge generation. Your answer should consider:

  • The benefits of automation in the data-information-knowledge hierarchy
  • Potential risks or limitations of automated systems
  • The impact on decision-making quality and speed
  • Cost-benefit analysis over a 2-year period

Make a justified recommendation on whether the investment should be made.

Command verb: Evaluate

Judge the importance or success of something using evidence. Review information and bring it together to form a conclusion, drawing on evidence including strengths, weaknesses, alternative actions, relevant data or information. Come to a supported judgement.
