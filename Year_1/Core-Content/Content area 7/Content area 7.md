Wi-Fi:
Wireless fidelity 
Wi-Fi is a standard in networking

Uses Radio waves Most commonly in the 2.4Ghz range and the 5Ghz range now pushing up to 6Ghz
We have had Wi-Fi 1 – 7 as of 03/09/2025 and Wi-Fi 8 is in the process of being developed
Based on the IEEE 802.11 Family of standards. These define the protocols of wireless Local area networks (WLANs)
IEEE = Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers

Pros: 
No need for cables, 
Easily accessible, 
Can support more users than Ethernet,
Costs less than running ethernet cables
Ease of expansion (allowing expanding the network to be done more easily)
Quicker to Deploy with less clutter
Boosts productivity as you can connect anywhere and use it rather than have to be plugged in via a cable


Cons: 
Lower security than ethernet,
Lower bandwidth,
Higher latency,	
Range
Microwaves and Water (Fishtanks) can block or interfere with the Signal

A phone in the collage classroom sends packets of information Via TCP or UDP to an access point or switch, it then sends it to the Router on the network. This is the main access to the internet and routs the packets towards the right place. Along with the packets it also send the Origin Ip address and the Destination Ip address of the Router at the other location (Australia) The packets then connect to routers which boost the signal, they will be sent to your ISPs data centre, they then get sent off to more routers to boost the signal so the packets make it to the other device, The packets will get sent across the Submarine cables to other countries like Australia, In the country they have a big “Exchange” which takes on those packets and sends them to the device in that country. When they reach the targets Router It will either use the Private IP address to connect to the persons device, or it will Use the MAC address to connect to the person device.

Bandwidth – maximum amount of data that can be sent via the transport medium
The more bandwidth you have the more Network intensive tasks you can do without Slowing one or the other down

Throughput – The Rate at which data is transferred through a system / transport medium

Latency – How long it takes to send and receive packets of data over the transmission media / internet
Factor that can affect latency are:
Cable length – The longer a cable the more signal degradation occurs, boosters are required to send it over long distances but fiber can travel further and way faster Than copper

Congestion (No. of users on a network) – This can add latency as more data is being sent around which means more interference over wireless and less priority to certain users.
