5.1 Business Environment

5.1.1 Know the purpose and sectors of different types of organisations
5.1.2 Know the names and definitions of different business models

Understanding how different organisations operate, their purposes, sectors, and business models is fundamental to working effectively in the digital technology industry.

Learning Objectives

📚 Organisational Knowledge

  • Understand the different purposes of organisations
  • Identify the key sectors organisations operate in
  • Recognise characteristics of different organisation types

💼 Business Model Knowledge

  • Define B2C, B2B, and B2M business models
  • Compare characteristics of each model
  • Identify real-world examples

Purpose of Organisations

All organisations exist to fulfill a specific purpose. The two primary purposes are:

🛍️

Providing a Product

Organisations that create, manufacture, or sell tangible goods that customers can own and use.

Examples:

Apple (smartphones, laptops), Nike (footwear, clothing), Tesla (electric vehicles), Unilever (consumer goods)

⚙️

Providing a Service

Organisations that deliver intangible benefits through expertise, labour, or technology to meet customer needs.

Examples:

Netflix (streaming), NHS (healthcare), Google (search/advertising), Deliveroo (food delivery)

Important Note: Many modern organisations provide both products AND services. For example, Amazon sells products but also provides cloud services (AWS), and Apple sells devices but also offers services like Apple Music and iCloud.

Overview of Organisational Sectors

Organisations are categorised into different sectors based on their ownership, funding, and objectives:

💼

Private Sector

Owned by individuals or shareholders. Aim to generate profit.

🏛️

Public Sector

Owned and operated by the government. Provide public services.

❤️

Voluntary/Charity

Not-for-profit. Focus on social, environmental, or community causes.

Private Sector: Small & Medium Enterprises (SMEs)

What is an SME?

A Small or Medium-sized Enterprise is a privately owned business that meets certain criteria for size, typically based on employee numbers and turnover.

Category Employees Annual Turnover Characteristics
Small Enterprise < 50 ≤ £10 million Local focus, owner-managed, flexible, limited resources
Medium Enterprise 50 - 249 ≤ £50 million Regional/national presence, growing team, established processes
UK Digital SME Examples:

Monzo Bank (fintech startup), Bulb Energy (energy supplier), local web design agencies, independent software development firms

Key Point: SMEs make up 99.9% of UK businesses and are crucial for innovation and employment in the digital sector.

Private Sector: Large Enterprises

What is a Large Enterprise?

A Large Enterprise typically has 250+ employees and annual turnover exceeding £50 million. These are often multinational corporations with complex structures.

Characteristics

  • Significant market share and influence
  • Multiple departments and hierarchical structure
  • Substantial financial resources
  • Global operations and supply chains
  • Established brand recognition
  • Formal processes and governance

Examples in Digital Sector

  • Microsoft - Software & Cloud Services
  • BT Group - Telecommunications
  • Amazon - E-commerce & AWS
  • Vodafone - Mobile Networks
  • Sky - Broadcasting & Broadband
  • Meta (Facebook) - Social Media

Impact on Digital Professionals: Large enterprises offer structured career paths, extensive training programs, and work on large-scale projects, but may have slower decision-making processes compared to SMEs.

Private Sector: Non-Governmental Organisations (NGOs)

What is an NGO?

A Non-Governmental Organisation operates independently of government control, often working on social, environmental, humanitarian, or advocacy issues. NGOs can be either not-for-profit or for-profit.

Key Features of NGOs

  • Independent from government
  • Focus on specific causes or issues
  • May operate nationally or internationally
  • Funded through donations, grants, or revenue
  • Stakeholder-focused rather than shareholder-focused

Types & Examples

International NGOs:

  • Oxfam (poverty relief)
  • Greenpeace (environmental)
  • Amnesty International (human rights)

Digital-focused NGOs:

  • Mozilla Foundation (internet health)
  • Electronic Frontier Foundation (digital rights)

Important Distinction: While many NGOs are not-for-profit, being an NGO is about governance structure and independence from government, not necessarily about profit status. Some NGOs generate revenue to sustain their operations.

Public Sector Organisations

What is the Public Sector?

The Public Sector consists of organisations owned and operated by the government (local or national) to provide essential services to citizens. Funded primarily through taxation.

Characteristics

  • Government ownership and control
  • Focus on public service, not profit
  • Accountable to taxpayers
  • Subject to political oversight
  • Universal access to services
  • Regulated by public sector rules

Examples in UK

  • NHS - National Health Service
  • BBC - Public Service Broadcaster
  • DVLA - Driver & Vehicle Licensing
  • HMRC - Tax Collection
  • Local Councils - Regional Services
  • Police Forces - Law Enforcement
  • State Schools - Education
Digital Technology in Public Sector:

The public sector is increasingly digital - think NHS App for healthcare bookings, GOV.UK for online government services, digital passport applications, and smart city initiatives by local councils.

Voluntary & Charity Sector (Not-for-Profit)

What is a Not-for-Profit Organisation?

Organisations in the Voluntary/Charity Sector exist to serve a social cause or community need rather than to generate profit for owners. Any surplus income is reinvested into the organisation's mission.

Key Characteristics

  • Mission-driven, not profit-driven
  • Reinvest surplus into cause
  • Funded by donations, grants, fundraising
  • Governed by board of trustees
  • Tax-exempt status (if registered charity)
  • Reliance on volunteers alongside paid staff

Examples

Large Charities:

  • Cancer Research UK
  • British Red Cross
  • Save the Children

Community Organisations:

  • Local food banks
  • Community centres

Digital Charities:

  • Wikipedia Foundation
  • Code.org

Comparing Sectors

Feature Private Sector Public Sector Voluntary/Charity
Primary Purpose Generate profit Provide public services Serve social cause
Ownership Private individuals, shareholders Government Independent, trustees
Funding Sales revenue, investment Taxation Donations, grants
Accountability Shareholders, customers Citizens, Parliament Donors, beneficiaries
Examples Amazon, Tesco, Barclays NHS, BBC, Councils Oxfam, RSPCA

5.1.2 Business Models

A business model describes who an organisation's customers are and how the organisation creates and delivers value to them.

Three primary business models in the digital economy:

🛒

B2C

Business to Customer

🏢

B2B

Business to Business

🌐

B2M

Business to Many

Important: Many organisations use multiple business models simultaneously. For example, Microsoft sells to individual consumers (B2C), enterprise clients (B2B), and operates platforms (B2M).

Business to Customer (B2C)

Definition

Business to Customer (B2C) is a business model where organisations sell products or services directly to individual consumers for their personal use.

Key Characteristics

  • Target: Individual consumers
  • Decisions: Emotional, personal needs
  • Transaction size: Smaller, frequent
  • Sales cycle: Short, immediate
  • Marketing: Brand awareness, social media
  • Relationship: Transactional
  • Volume: High number of customers

Digital B2C Examples

E-commerce:

Amazon, ASOS, eBay

Streaming:

Netflix, Spotify, Disney+

Food Delivery:

Deliveroo, Just Eat, Uber Eats

Banking:

Monzo, Revolut

Business to Business (B2B)

Definition

Business to Business (B2B) is a business model where organisations sell products or services to other businesses rather than individual consumers.

Key Characteristics

  • Target: Other businesses
  • Decisions: Rational, ROI-focused
  • Transaction size: Larger contracts
  • Sales cycle: Long, complex
  • Marketing: Industry events, LinkedIn
  • Relationship: Long-term partnerships
  • Volume: Fewer, higher-value customers

Digital B2B Examples

Cloud Services:

AWS, Microsoft Azure, Google Cloud

Software/SaaS:

Salesforce, Slack, Adobe

Cybersecurity:

CrowdStrike, Cisco

Payment Processing:

Stripe, PayPal Business

Business to Many (B2M)

Definition

Business to Many (B2M) is a business model where organisations create platforms or marketplaces that connect multiple parties - enabling transactions between consumers, businesses, or both simultaneously.

Key Characteristics

  • Platform model: Connects multiple users
  • Network effects: Value increases with users
  • Revenue: Commissions, subscriptions, ads
  • Dual customers: Buyers and sellers
  • Scalability: Rapid growth potential
  • Technology-dependent: Robust infrastructure
  • Community building: Engagement focus

Digital B2M Examples

E-commerce Platforms:

Amazon Marketplace, eBay

Ride-Sharing:

Uber, Lyft

Social Media:

Facebook, Instagram

Freelance Platforms:

Upwork, Fiverr

Comparing Business Models

Feature B2C B2B B2M
Customer Individual consumers Other businesses Multiple parties
Decision Emotional, personal Rational, ROI-focused Varies by user type
Value Lower, frequent Higher, bulk Variable, commission
Sales Cycle Short, immediate Long, complex Platform facilitates
Volume High volume Lower volume Very high (both sides)
Examples Netflix, Amazon retail Salesforce, AWS Uber, Airbnb, eBay

Exam-Style Question 1

Knowledge Question

(a) Define what is meant by a B2M (Business to Many) business model. [2 marks]

(b) Explain how a B2M business model differs from a B2C model. [3 marks]

(c) Give two examples of digital companies using B2M and explain how they create value. [3 marks]

[8 marks total]

Mark Scheme

(a) 2 marks:

  • B2M creates platforms/marketplaces (1 mark)
  • That connect multiple parties to facilitate transactions (1 mark)

(b) 3 marks:

  • B2C sells directly to consumers, B2M creates platform connecting users (1 mark)
  • B2C owns/provides product, B2M facilitates others to transact (1 mark)
  • B2M has network effects, value increases with more users (1 mark)

(c) 3 marks:

  • Uber - connects drivers/passengers, convenient matching (1.5 marks)
  • Airbnb - connects property owners/travelers (1.5 marks)
  • Award 1 mark for company, 0.5 for value creation

Exam-Style Question 2

Extended Response Question

Scenario: TechStartup Ltd is a small software development company with 35 employees in Manchester. They create mobile apps for local businesses and charge for development and maintenance.

Question: Identify and explain the sector, size category, and business model. Justify your answer with reference to the scenario.

[8 marks]

Mark Scheme

  • Sector: Private sector - "Ltd" indicates private ownership (1 mark)
  • Size: Small enterprise (SME) - 35 employees < 50 (1 mark)
  • Purpose: Provides service - software development (1 mark)
  • Business model: B2B - clients are "local businesses" (2 marks)
  • Justification: Evidence includes private ownership, employee count, service-based, business clients (2-3 marks for comprehensive justification)

Exam-Style Question 3

Analysis Question

Scenario: The NHS is launching a digital health platform for patients to book appointments and access records. HealthTech Solutions (private sector) is contracted to develop it.

Question: Compare the sectors and business models of NHS and HealthTech Solutions. Explain how their different characteristics might affect their approach to this project.

[10 marks]

Mark Scheme

  • NHS: Public sector, government-funded, B2C (serving citizens), accountable to taxpayers (2 marks)
  • HealthTech: Private sector, profit-driven, B2B (selling to NHS), accountable to shareholders (2 marks)
  • Business model impact: NHS B2C citizen-focused, HealthTech B2B business client-focused (1-2 marks)
  • Project approach: NHS prioritises patient welfare, security, accessibility; HealthTech focuses on contract requirements, profitability (2-3 marks)
  • Collaboration: Public needs combined with private efficiency (1-2 marks)

Exam-Style Question 4

Evaluation Question

Scenario: Maya has three job offers:

  • CloudCorp - Large enterprise, £45k, B2B cloud infrastructure
  • Digital Dreams - Startup (15 employees), £38k + equity, B2M app marketplace
  • CodeForGood - Charity (50 employees), £35k, educational tech (B2B/B2C)

Question: Evaluate which organisation Maya should choose. Consider types, sectors, business models and how these affect career development, job security, and work environment. Justify your recommendation.

[12 marks]

Mark Scheme

  • CloudCorp: Large enterprise advantages (career progression, training, security) vs disadvantages (slower pace, less impact) (2-3 marks)
  • Digital Dreams: Startup advantages (innovation, equity, responsibility) vs disadvantages (risk, security, hours) (2-3 marks)
  • CodeForGood: Charity advantages (mission-driven, impact) vs disadvantages (salary, resources) (2-3 marks)
  • Business model considerations: B2B enterprise experience, B2M platform skills, hybrid versatility (1-2 marks)
  • Recommendation: Justified choice based on goals, values, needs (2-3 marks)

Note: Any choice can be justified - marks for quality of reasoning.
