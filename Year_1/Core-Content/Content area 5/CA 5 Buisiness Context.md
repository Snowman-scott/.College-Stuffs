## Once we have an understanding of these industries, we can start to look at the impact IT has had on each one.

**Primary:** British Sugar has embraced IT through a partnership with IBM to create a data-driven organisation powered by AI. They have developed one of the first digital twins in sugar manufacturing, allowing real-time monitoring and predictive analysis of their supply chain. This has led to a 20% reduction in time spent manually understanding data, 25% faster decision-making, and a 50% decrease in operational setbacks. They have also deployed a private 4G network across their four UK factories (managed by Virgin Media O2 Business) to enable real-time monitoring, predictive maintenance, and cutting-edge robotics. An automated storage and retrieval system at their Wissington refinery has boosted throughput speed and improved safety by minimising forklift traffic.

**Secondary:** Jaguar Land Rover has integrated IT extensively into their vehicles and manufacturing. Starting in 2025, all new Jaguar and Land Rover vehicles are built on the NVIDIA DRIVE software-defined platform, delivering advanced driver assistance systems (ADAS), automated driving, and parking systems. Their on-board ADAS processes radar and camera data every 20 milliseconds to keep drivers in lane, maintain safe distances, and avoid accidents. JLR is developing their Operating System in-house and uses NVIDIA DGX data centre solutions for training AI models and DRIVE Sim software for real-time simulation. They have invested $180 million in their North American technology hub and opened three new tech hubs in Europe (Munich, Bologna, Madrid) to develop autonomous driving systems.

**Tertiary:** Tesco PLC has implemented IT solutions including their "Scan as you Shop" service, which allows Clubcard members to scan items with handheld devices while shopping, pack directly into bags, and pay quickly at dedicated checkouts. This service is now available in 359 stores, with over 10 million transactions per month. Customers can also use the Tesco app to build shopping lists, unlock handsets, and tick off items as they scan. Tesco has also introduced self-service checkouts and is testing scan-free kiosks at some stores where consumers can review their items and pay without scanning each product. These IT implementations have improved inventory management, streamlined supply chain operations, and enhanced the customer experience.

**Public:** The NHS has leveraged IT through its Frontline Digitisation programme, achieving the target of 90% of trusts adopting electronic patient record (EPR) systems. NHS England invested over 400 million to support 150 NHS trusts in implementing EPRs, with 189 trusts now using modern record systems. Evidence shows EPRs have led to a 3.5 percentage point reduction in sepsis mortality for surgical inpatients. Telemedicine has grown significantly, especially after COVID-19, with initiatives like "GP in the Cloud" enabling secure remote consultations across the UK. Virtual wards (hospital-at-home) are being scaled up, and the NHS App provides consolidated views of patient records, test results, appointments, and prescriptions to both patients and clinicians.

### **How IT Supports Business Functions**

**Human Resources:** The HR function manages the employee lifecycle, from recruitment to offboarding. IT is fundamental to modern HR, automating administrative tasks and providing data-driven insights. This is achieved with a foundation of standard office hardware like **laptops** and **servers** (both on-premise and cloud-based) running specialised software. **Human Resources Information Systems (HRIS)** such as **Workday** or **SAP SuccessFactors** act as a central database for employee records, payroll, and benefits. Recruitment is streamlined through **Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS)**, while employee training is delivered and tracked via **Learning Management Systems (LMS)**. This digital transformation allows HR professionals to focus less on paperwork and more on strategic workforce planning.

**Research, Design and Development:** R&D is the engine of innovation, responsible for creating new products and improving existing ones. IT accelerates this process by providing powerful tools for simulation and analysis. The hardware backbone includes **high-performance workstations** with powerful **NVIDIA Quadro GPUs** for running **Computer-Aided Design (CAD)** software like **SolidWorks** or **AutoCAD**. These systems allow engineers to create and test digital prototypes before incurring manufacturing costs. For complex data analysis and AI model training, R&D teams rely on **High-Performance Computing (HPC) clusters**. The entire process is managed and versioned using collaboration software like **Jira** for project management and **Git** for source control, enabling global teams to work together efficiently.

**Logistics:** As the part of the supply chain that manages the flow and storage of goods, logistics is critically dependent on IT for efficiency and visibility. The process is tracked using a network of hardware, including **handheld barcode scanners**, **mobile computers**, and **GPS trackers** on vehicles. This hardware feeds real-time data into sophisticated software systems. A **Warehouse Management System (WMS)** optimizes storage, picking, and packing within the warehouse, while a **Transportation Management System (TMS)** plans the most efficient shipping routes and manages carriers. These systems are often integrated within a larger **Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP)** system like **SAP S/4HANA** to ensure data flows seamlessly from an initial order to final delivery.

**Marketing:** The marketing function is responsible for identifying and satisfying customer needs to drive sales. In the digital age, marketing is almost entirely reliant on IT. Day-to-day work is done on standard **laptops** and **mobile devices**, but the power lies in the software. **Customer Relationship Management (CRM)** systems like **Salesforce** are the central hub for all customer data. Marketers use **Marketing Automation Platforms** like **HubSpot** to run email campaigns and **Social Media Management Tools** such as **Hootsuite** to manage online presence. The effectiveness of these campaigns is tracked using analytics platforms like **Google Analytics**. All of this customer and campaign data is stored on secure **servers** or **cloud infrastructure** like AWS.

**Finance:** The finance department manages an organisation's monetary resources, handling everything from accounting to financial planning. IT supports this function by automating processes, ensuring data accuracy, and enhancing security. The core of the finance department's IT toolkit is **accounting software** (e.g., **QuickBooks**, **Xero**) or a finance module within a larger **ERP system** like **Oracle NetSuite**. This software runs on secure **workstations** and **servers**. To protect highly sensitive financial data, IT implements robust **cybersecurity software** and often uses **Hardware Security Modules (HSMs)** to secure cryptographic keys for transactions. For analysis and reporting, finance teams use **Business Intelligence (BI) tools** like **Microsoft Power BI** to create dashboards and visualise financial health in real-time.

**Management:** The management function is responsible for planning, organising, leading, and controlling company resources to achieve organisational goals. IT provides management with the tools for oversight, communication, and strategic decision-making. Managers rely on **laptops**, **smartphones**, and **video conferencing equipment** to communicate with their teams. They gain a high-level view of the entire business through dashboards in **ERP systems** and **CRM software**, which consolidate data from all other functions. **Project management software** like **Asana** or **Trello** is used to assign tasks and monitor project progress. By providing access to real-time data and facilitating seamless communication, IT enables managers to make more informed decisions and lead more effectively.
