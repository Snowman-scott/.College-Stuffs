What is Agile?

Agile is a mindset and philosophy for software development that emphasises flexibility, collaboration, and customer satisfaction.

🎯 Core Concept

Rather than planning everything upfront and delivering at the end, Agile focuses on:

  • Iterative development – Building software in small, manageable increments
  • Continuous feedback – Regular input from stakeholders and users
  • Adaptive planning – Responding to change rather than rigidly following a plan
  • Collaboration – Close communication between developers, customers, and stakeholders

💡 Think of Agile as building a house room-by-room, getting feedback after each room, rather than waiting until the entire house is built to show anyone!

The Agile Manifesto (2001)

Created by 17 software developers, the Agile Manifesto outlines four key values:

👥 Individuals & Interactions

Over processes and tools

💻 Working Software

Over comprehensive documentation

🤝 Customer Collaboration

Over contract negotiation

🔄 Responding to Change

Over following a plan

Important: While the items on the right have value, Agile values the items on the left more.

What is Scrum?

Scrum is the most popular framework for implementing Agile principles.

🏉 Why "Scrum"?

The name comes from rugby, where the team works together in a unified, coordinated effort to move the ball forward.

⚙️ Scrum Framework

Scrum provides a structured yet flexible framework with:

  • Defined roles – Clear responsibilities for team members
  • Time-boxed events – Regular ceremonies to maintain rhythm
  • Artefacts – Tangible items that represent work and value
  • Rules and guidelines – Best practices for team success

Scrum helps teams deliver working software frequently (typically every 2-4 weeks) whilst continuously improving their process.

Scrum Roles

Scrum defines three key roles, each with distinct responsibilities:

📋 Product Owner

The Vision Keeper

  • Defines product features
  • Prioritises the backlog
  • Accepts or rejects work
  • Represents stakeholders

🎯 Scrum Master

The Process Facilitator

  • Ensures Scrum is followed
  • Removes impediments
  • Facilitates ceremonies
  • Protects the team

👨‍💻 Development Team

The Builders

  • Self-organising
  • Cross-functional
  • Typically 3-9 members
  • Delivers the increment

Scrum Events (Ceremonies)

Scrum uses time-boxed events to create regularity and minimise unplanned meetings:

🎯 Sprint Planning

Duration: Up to 8 hours (for 4-week sprint)

Team plans the work for the upcoming sprint. They select items from the Product Backlog and create a Sprint Goal.

📅 Daily Scrum

Duration: 15 minutes

Daily stand-up meeting. Each member answers: What did I do yesterday? What will I do today? Any blockers?

🎨 Sprint Review

Duration: Up to 4 hours (for 4-week sprint)

Team demonstrates completed work to stakeholders. Gather feedback and update the Product Backlog.

🔍 Sprint Retrospective

Duration: Up to 3 hours (for 4-week sprint)

Team reflects on the sprint process. Discuss what went well, what didn't, and plan improvements.

The Sprint Cycle

⏱️ Typical 2-Week Sprint Timeline

Day 1: Sprint Planning 🎯
Days 2-10: Development Work + Daily Scrums 💻
Day 10: Sprint Review 🎨
Day 10: Sprint Retrospective 🔍
Repeat! The next sprint begins immediately 🔄

✨ Key Points

  • Fixed duration: Sprints are typically 1-4 weeks (2 weeks is most common)
  • No scope changes: Once started, sprint scope is frozen
  • Potentially shippable: Each sprint should produce working software
  • Continuous cycle: Sprints run back-to-back with no gaps

Scrum Artefacts

Scrum defines three primary artefacts that provide transparency and opportunities for inspection:

📚 Product Backlog

Owned by: Product Owner

  • Ordered list of everything needed in the product
  • Single source of requirements
  • Constantly evolving and refined
  • Items at top are more detailed

📝 Sprint Backlog

Owned by: Development Team

  • Items selected for the sprint
  • Plus the plan to deliver them
  • Visible real-time picture of work
  • Updated throughout the sprint

✅ Increment

Result of: Sprint

  • Sum of all completed backlog items
  • Must meet "Definition of Done"
  • Must be in usable condition
  • Potentially releasable

Benefits of Using Scrum

✅ For the Team

  • Clear goals: Focus on sprint objectives and deliverables
  • Regular feedback: Continuous improvement through retrospectives
  • Sustainable pace: Avoid burnout with time-boxed sprints
  • Empowerment: Self-organising teams make their own decisions

✅ For the Product

  • Faster time-to-market: Regular releases of working software
  • Higher quality: Continuous testing and integration
  • Better requirements: Regular feedback refines what's needed
  • Adaptability: Easy to change direction based on feedback

✅ For the Business

  • Visibility: Regular sprint reviews show progress
  • Predictability: Velocity helps forecast delivery dates
  • Risk reduction: Early detection of problems
  • ROI: Prioritise high-value features first

Agile vs Traditional Waterfall

🌊 Waterfall (Traditional)

  • Sequential phases
  • Extensive upfront planning
  • Requirements fixed early
  • Testing at the end
  • One big release
  • Change is difficult
  • Customer sees product at end

🔄 Agile/Scrum

  • Iterative cycles (sprints)
  • Just-enough planning
  • Requirements evolve
  • Testing throughout
  • Frequent small releases
  • Change is welcomed
  • Regular customer involvement

🎯 When to Use Each?

Waterfall: Suitable for projects with well-defined, stable requirements (e.g., regulatory systems, construction)

Agile/Scrum: Ideal for projects with evolving requirements, innovation, and frequent change (most software projects!)

Real-World Example: Building a Mobile App

📱 Scenario: Student Study Planner App

Your team needs to build a study planning app for students. Here's how Scrum would work:

Sprint 1 (2 weeks) - MVP

  • User authentication (login/register)
  • Basic task list
  • Add/delete tasks

Review: Stakeholders test it, provide feedback: "We need due dates!"

Sprint 2 (2 weeks) - Enhance

  • Add due dates to tasks
  • Calendar view
  • Task priority levels

Review: Feedback: "Can we get notifications for upcoming deadlines?"

Sprint 3 (2 weeks) - Expand

  • Push notifications
  • Task categories (exams, assignments, revision)
  • Progress tracking

Each sprint delivers working software that users can test. Requirements adapt based on real feedback!

Key Takeaways

🎯 Remember These Core Concepts

  • Agile is a mindset – Embrace change, collaboration, and continuous improvement
  • Scrum is a framework – Provides structure with roles, events, and artefacts
  • Sprints are time-boxed – Typically 2 weeks, produce working software
  • Three roles: Product Owner, Scrum Master, Development Team
  • Four ceremonies: Sprint Planning, Daily Scrum, Sprint Review, Sprint Retrospective
  • Three artefacts: Product Backlog, Sprint Backlog, Increment
